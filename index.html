A material framework for biology

Life is built from atoms.
Biology should begin there.

Atom-first Biology investigates how environmental elemental availability becomes organized into living systems—and how that material organization shapes physiology, ecology, evolution, and disease.

Explore the framework
The framework

Organisms are dynamic multielement systems.

Every organism must acquire chemical elements from its environment, organize them into a functioning biochemical system, and continually reorganize that system as conditions change.

Atom-first Biology starts with this material reality. It asks how geochemistry, elemental acquisition, ionomic organization, and biochemical allocation connect environment to phenotype and evolution.

Geology→ Environment→ Acquisition→ Ionome→ Phenotype→ Evolution

What is atom-first biology?

  • Starts with elements, not organisms, traits, molecules, or nucleotides.
  • Treats biological systems—from organelles and cells to populations and communities—as dynamical compositional systems.
  • Analyzes inorganic biochemical systems in the context of the geochemical systems in which they are embedded.
  • Gives equal attention to numerator and denominator chemistry.
  • Quantifies elemental proportions to connect atom-scale organization to higher-order biological and ecological phenomena.

This perspective is not a new field. It is a call to bring established knowledge from inorganic chemistry and biophysical systems into mainstream biological questions.

About

A research program, not a collection of organisms.

Atom-first Biology is an interdisciplinary program integrating physiology, ecology, evolution, geochemistry, and compositional biology. Diverse experimental systems are used to develop and test the same underlying material framework.

The program is led by Puni D. Jeyasingh in the Department of Integrative Biology at Oklahoma State University.

Research approach

Begin with material composition.

Atom-first biology draws from bioinorganic chemistry and biogeochemistry, and is inspired by ecological stoichiometry, ionomics, R.J.P. Williams’ biological chemistry, and Lotka’s physical biology.

We quantify elemental composition of environments—water, soil, and diet—and biological material across scales from cells to populations. We combine theory, field and laboratory experiments, and surveys to study how elemental supply becomes biochemical organization.

Even when experiments manipulate one nutrient, biomass may respond through wider ionomic imbalances (Jeyasingh et al. 2020, 2023). The question is when measuring that broader material state changes ecological inference.

Elemental data quality depends on preparation, contamination control, calibration, and matrix effects. Samples are analyzed in-house or with specialized facilities including Cove Sciences.

Teaching

Wonder, confidence, and independent thought.

The central goal of my teaching and mentoring is to instill wonder and confidence by emphasizing what we do not yet understand, rather than only what is already known. Clear thinking and communication are central to every course.

BIOL 2003 — Biology for the Informed Citizen

General education science for non-biology majors, emphasizing scientific thinking and biological knowledge relevant to everyday life. Offered every spring and in fall of odd years.

BIOL 5133 — Evolutionary Ecology

A graduate course integrating ecological and evolutionary theory. Offered in fall of even years.

Mentoring emphasizes curiosity, independent inquiry, careful writing, and science conducted with selfless passion.

Philosophy

Curiosity leads us to science. Doubt drives it.

An atom-first perspective should be thrilling to new biologists and humbling to accomplished ones. It places human discovery within the context of atom-scale complexity and the limitations of perception and intuition.

Biology advanced through dissection and classification. These approaches remain powerful, but their limitations must now be examined in the light of biophysical reality (Lotka 1925; Prigogine 1978).
