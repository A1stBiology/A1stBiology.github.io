The framework · From the original Google Site

Biology, beginning with elements.

Atom-first biology starts with elements, not organisms, traits, molecules, or nucleotides.

It treats biosystems (e.g., organelles, cells, populations, communities) as dynamical compositional systems and analyzes inorganic biochemical systems in the context of the geochemical systems in which they are embedded.

Numerator and denominator

Equal attention to numerator and denominator chemistry.

Composition across scales

Quantify the relative proportions of chemical elements in biological systems, from cells to lakes, to understand biology as an integrated compositional system at its lowest level of organization.

Compositional data

Elemental data are inherently compositional. Treating them as such allows us to connect detailed, often modular knowledge of individual elements to prediction of higher-order biological and ecological phenomena.

An elemental perspective

We call this elemental, rather than organismal, perspective atom-first biology.

About atom-first biology

Not a new field. A different starting point.

Atom-first biology is not a new field. It is a reminder that imperceptible atom-scale processes shape biological systems at every scale.

It is also a call to leverage longstanding knowledge from inorganic chemistry and biophysical systems that has remained ancillary to mainstream biology.

This perspective may be relevant to ecologists integrating across scales; physiologists interested in material constraints; toxicologists studying elemental interactions; statisticians analyzing compositional systems; and mathematicians seeking unifying, scale-invariant principles in biology.
